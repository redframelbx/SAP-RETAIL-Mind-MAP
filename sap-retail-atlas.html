Identifying the Organizational Structures

Financial & Controlling

  • Client: corporate group, may be subdivided into subsidiaries
  • Company Code: independent unit that balances accounts per legal requirements
  • Controlling Area: subdivides the business for cost accounting
  • Cost Centers: units within controlling areas — defined cost incurrence locations
  • Operating Concern: structures the enterprise for profitability analysis across market segments
  • Profit Center: branch/division accounted for independently for profit calculation

Purchasing Structures

  • Purchasing Organization: procures for multiple stores, negotiates vendor conditions — a data retention level
  • Purchasing Group: one or more buyers; maintains master data & purchasing activity (not a data retention level)
  • Purchasing Area: optional connector of orgs & groups, for reporting
Organizational StructuresSAP S/4HANA Retail

Inventory Management

  • Site: a store, distribution center, or production location
  • Storage Location: stock-keeping unit with its own inventory management
  • Distribution Center: usually split into multiple storage locations; can link to warehouse management
  • Store: typically assigned one storage location

Sales & Distribution

  • Distribution Chain: sales organization + distribution channel
  • Sales Organization: legally responsible for sales & product liability
  • Distribution Channels: market approaches — store chains, wholesale, mail order
  • Division: SD key field, usually a dummy value (e.g. "R1") in retail

Strategic vs. Operational

  • Strategic: company codes, purchasing organizations
    Require customization before master data creation
  • Operational: purchasing groups
    No data retention function — support daily activities

Exploring the Business Partner, Retail Sites, and Article Groupings

Business Partner Concept

  • 3 BP categories: Natural Person, Group, Organization — retail partners are "Organization"
  • Central transaction: BP – Edit Business Partner

Customer Master

  • General BP (role 000000): client-wide data — address, banking
  • CVI: FI Customer (role FLCU00): accounting at company code level
  • CVI: Customer (role FLCU01): sales/distribution data per sales area
  • Every site has an internal customer master record (debit-side BP)

Supplier Master

  • General BP (role 000000): address, banking
  • CVI: FI Vendor (role FLVN00): accounting at company code level
  • CVI: Vendor (role FLVN01): purchasing data — order currency, incoterms
  • Suppliers can be blocked for quality/product-specific reasons

Site Definition & Categories

  • Site: org. unit proving evidence of merchandise stored in DCs/stores for inventory management
  • Belongs to one company code; maintains its own customer master (integrated BP)
  • Stores (Category A): display & sell to consumers at the sales floor
  • Distribution Centers (Category B): hold stock supplying other sites or external customers
  • Distribution chain category: consumer/retail chains (stores) vs. wholesale/DC chains
  • Department stores (store cat. 1) & shops (store cat. 2): both site category A — map shop‑in‑shop / mall scenarios

Internal Customer/Supplier Relationships

  • DCs act as both internal customers and suppliers
  • As customer: receive goods from higher-level DCs, or returns from stores/customers
  • As supplier: provide merchandise to stores and other sites
  • Dual role needs both customer & supplier master records — especially cross‑company
Business Partner, Sites & Article GroupingsSAP S/4HANA Retail

Merchandise Category Hierarchy

  • Every article assigned to one merchandise category
  • Mandatory structure, based on SAP classification system, class type 026
  • Reference article: template dummy article with default values for new article masters
  • Value-only articles: category-level or hierarchy-level, for value-based inventory mgmt (e.g. promo clearance)
  • Characteristic inheritance: lower levels inherit from higher levels — enables analysis/reporting
  • Categories assignable to sites for site-specific settings (pricing, replenishment, valuation)
  • Supplying site(s) can be defined at site/merchandise-category level, overriding general site assignment (e.g. perishables DC)

Article Hierarchy (AH)

  • Alternative grouping — used for assortment planning
  • Merchandise category = material attributes; AH = sales aspects (brands, sales-floor presentation, packaging)
  • Customizable depth (10 levels recommended); asymmetrical arrangements allowed
  • Can mirror physical shop layout / floor sections
  • Optional seasonal scheduling via validity periods
  • Lifecycle: starts "Planned" → "Active" after system checks
    Once activated, an article hierarchy always remains active
  • High-level nodes = category levels (root of consumer decision trees); each category needs ≥1 assortment
  • With multiple article assignment on, articles appear once per category

Introducing the Retail Article Master

Core Concept & Data Organization

  • Article master = comprehensive object consolidating data across all business functions
  • Single integrated maintenance transaction spans planning, purchasing, logistics, sales
  • Contains descriptive (size, weight), control (article type), and system-maintained (stock, valuation) data

Views (departments)

  • Basic Data, Listing, Purchasing, Sales, Logistics (DC & Store), POS

Data retention levels

  • Basic Data: client-wide (article number only)
  • Listing: distribution chain level
  • Purchasing: purchasing org. & supplier level
  • Sales: distribution chain min., optional site/price-list detail
  • Logistics: client-wide or site-specific
  • POS: distribution chain required, site-specific optional

Article Categories

  • Single Articles: standard items sold as-is, possibly multiple units of measure
  • Generic Articles w/ Variants: share generic-level data; variants differ by characteristic (color, size, flavor) and act as "operational articles"
    Needs: characteristics w/ values, configuration class (type 300), variant matrix — up to 5 variant-creating characteristics
  • Structured Articles (header + BOM components):
    Display · Sales Set · Purchasing Set · Prepack (variants, same merchandise category)
  • Empties Articles: returnable packaging linked to full products per UoM, own bill of materials

Units of Measure

  • Base Unit: inventory management standard
  • Order Unit: per-supplier purchasing unit
  • Sales Unit: customer transaction unit
  • Delivery/Issue Unit: DC shipment unit
  • Conversion factors link all units to base/alternative units
Retail Article MasterSAP S/4HANA Retail

Article Configuration

  • Screen sequences: 24 (food/non-food), F4 (fashion) — control views/subscreens shown
  • Selectable by transaction, user, article type, or industry
  • Field status management: hide, display, required, optional

SAP Fiori — Manage Product Master Data

  • Search & filtering, draft auto-save, copy w/ org. data selection, image management, cross-app navigation

Creation & Maintenance Methods

  • Reference articles: merchandise-category reference (auto defaults) or explicit reference (overrides) — excludes GTINs, consumption/forecast values, prices/conditions
  • Core article function: rapid creation via focused screens, full data deferred
  • Article copy: duplicates data segments (all or restricted), excludes pricing references & GTINs
  • GDS (Global Data Sync): supplier data via GDSN data pools
    Subscription → Data Inbound/Validation (CIN) → Data Sync (flexible rules) → Confirmation (CIC). Supports single, generic & structured articles
  • Mass Maintenance: Integrated Mass Change (WRFMASSMAT) via maintenance groups; templates for default org. data

Discontinuation, Archiving & Article Lists

  • Partial discontinuation: removes listing conditions/supplier relationships, keeps master & stock
  • Complete discontinuation: deletes full article record + related data after retention period
  • Strategy: avoid unnecessary data creation, compress at higher org. levels, archive systematically
    Scale: ~10⁵ articles × 10³ stores ≈ 10⁸ data points
  • Article lists: dynamic (saved selection criteria) vs. static (manually selected)
  • Status levels: Planned, Released, Released/Not Modifiable

Introducing Assortment Management

Assortment Types & Structure

  • Assortment Mgmt = creating assortments + executing article listing; assigns articles to sites via listing conditions

Local Assortments (Cat. A/B)

  • Auto-created with each site; site-specific, not reassignable
  • Cat. A = stores, Cat. B = distribution centers
  • Technical key = site's customer number; exactly one per site

General Assortments (Cat. C)

  • Assignable to multiple SD customers/sales areas; "multiple assignment" links many stores/DCs
  • Good for regional/seasonal groups, simplifying new site setup, reducing listing-condition volume
  • Created manually, or via planning tool store-clustering (sales history, margin, revenue, custom attributes)

Reference Sites

  • Group sites for simplified assignment — assign the reference site instead of each site individually
  • A site can't be assigned twice to one assortment, but can be direct to one and indirect (via reference) to another

Listing & Listing Procedures

  • Assortment = object articles are assigned/"listed" to, for a validity period
  • Listing conditions valid only for the assigned users — single site (local) or designated users (general)
  • Valid listing = prerequisite for article availability in planning, ordering, receiving, selling

3 Listing Procedures

  • B1 — Merchandise Category/Grade Check: verifies category assignment; optional grade differentiates by store size (e.g. small = basic dairy, large = broad range)
  • B2 — Classification Check: lists only if article characteristics/values match assortment parameters
  • B3 — Layout Check: confirms article's layout module linked to the assortment/site (directly or via layout)
  • Procedures can be combined/customized

Category management example

  • 3 assortments by store size, each carrying seasonal chocolate/flavor rotations (e.g. summer vs. winter flavors) via non-overlapping layout module versions

Layout Workbench

  • Central platform for shelf optimization; select a site/assortment to see layouts, modules, versions, and run listing
  • Assign/modify article assignments; mass assignment in single operations
  • Run listing procedures
  • Maintain layout module variants (local swaps for regional specialties)
  • Display/assign fixtures (shelves, gondolas, baskets)
  • Display planogram files; access external shelf-optimization programs
  • Manual article placement supported without external integration
  • Layout connection: auto-created with general assortment, or manually assigned

Executing Retail Pricing

Core Concept & Pricing Hierarchy

  • Addresses managing sales prices across large inventories with multiple pricing tiers
  • Price creation/maintenance automated via markup conditions & change pointer functionality
3 pricing levels (general → specific): Distribution chain → Distribution chain w/ price list → Site
  • Hierarchical lookup: site-specific price wins; else price list; else distribution chain default

Key Capabilities

  • Manual adjustments: change calculation data — cost price, markup, final price — in sales price calculations
  • Pricing functions: family pricing (related-article grouping), competitor pricing (external competitive strategies)
  • Markup structure: planned markups increase purchase price — combine cost components + profit targets, typically defined by sales org., distribution channel, merchandise category
Executing Retail PricingSAP S/4HANA Retail

Display Configuration

  • List Groups (A–D): control whether articles/org. levels show at header vs. item level; variants C & D use ALV table design
  • List Variants: control which pricing-table columns appear and their sequence

Calculation Models

  • One-Step: direct vendor → store pricing from external purchase prices
  • Two-Step: DC calculates transfer price from vendor cost → store calculates consumer price from DC transfer price
  • Backward Calculation: reverse pricing from a fixed target price — auto-computes gross/net margins

Price Maintenance

  • Change pointers auto-monitor cost fluctuations & master data changes
  • Pricing worklist evaluates these indicators and recommends retail price adjustments

Performing Requirements Planning

Planning Foundations

  • Goal: guarantee article availability at each site while optimizing service level and minimizing costs / capital lockup
  • Planning happens at article/site level, supporting both centralized and localized purchasing
  • Key parameters: MRP type, lot sizing procedure, MRP controller, forecast intervals & historical data range

Forecasting Models

  • Constant, trend, seasonal, and seasonal-trend models
  • Each needs a minimum amount of consumption history to run

Three Planning Procedures

  • Reorder Point Planning: triggers a purchase requisition once stock drops below a threshold
    Reorder point = safety stock + expected consumption during replenishment lead time (purchasing + delivery + goods receipt time)
  • Time-Phased Planning: used when vendors deliver on a fixed schedule — needs a planning calendar, delivery cycle, and delivery time set up
  • Forecast-Based Planning: projects future consumption from history and feeds that forecast into the planning run as required quantity

MRP Live Transaction

  • The main tool for running requirements planning; scope is chosen by MRP controller, site, or other selection criteria
  • Regenerative Planning: plan all articles vs. only changed ones
  • Planning Mode: reuse or recreate unfirmed procurement proposals
  • Runs at MRP Area level (typically site), checks the planning file, and performs an ATP check against current stock and existing documents
Requirements Planning SAP S/4HANA Retail

Planned Independent Requirements

  • Used for seasonal merchandise on generic articles and variants
  • PIRs are planned quantities for an article/site within a defined time horizon
  • Created manually or imported from SAP Assortment Planning
  • Fed into MRP runs together with sales orders, stock transfers, and current inventory

Purchase Requisitions & Auto Conversion

  • Requisitions carry a proposed quantity and delivery date, derived from past consumption and current/projected stock
  • Can generate an RFQ, or convert straight into a PO when a supply source already exists

Automatic PO Conversion needs all three

  • Site master flags "automatic order conversion"
  • Material master allows "automatic purchase order"
  • Supplier master authorizes "automatic purchase order"
Planning run → Purchase requisition → Source determination → (optional) Auto conversion & approval → Purchase order

Supply Source Determination

  • MRP Live sequence (external): quota arrangements → outline agreements → info records (auto-source) → info records (regular vendor)
  • Retail-specific sequence: quota arrangements → source lists → Supply Source field on article master → outline agreements
  • Internal sourcing: stock transport scheduling agreements first, then the supplying site set in the site master

Outline Agreements

  • Value contract: fulfilled at an agreed monetary value
  • Quantity contract: fulfilled at an agreed quantity
  • Scheduling agreement: regular, recurring deliveries
  • Group-wide contracts can apply across all sites/company codes, with site-specific conditions still possible

Setting Up Purchase Order Management

PO Structure & Org Attributes

  • A purchase order splits into three areas: Header (org/document-level data), Item Overview (all line items), and Item Detail (per-item detail)
  • Views can be shown alone, or paired: header + item overview, or item overview + item details

Required Assignments

  • Purchasing organization
  • Purchasing group
  • Company code
  • Order type — e.g. NB (standard order), UB (stock transport order)

Creation Methods

  • Manual creation
  • Automatic conversion from a purchase requisition
  • Store Replenishment runs
  • POS Inbound Store Order function
  • Allocation table follow-on documents
  • External interfaces, e.g. SAP Forecasting and Replenishment

Header & Item Functions

Header Level

  • Texts, partner functions, supplier addresses, conditions, and access to the supplier master record

Item Level

  • Condition management, variant matrix ordering, returns items, empty product displays, structured article components, free goods items, schedule line maintenance
Purchase Order Management SAP S/4HANA Retail

Quantity Optimizing

  • Adjusts requisition quantities using each article's rounding profile
  • Considers minimum/maximum order quantities, rounding to set multiples, and alternative units of measure
  • System always tries to order in the largest sensible unit to get the best pricing conditions
Example: units are each / case / layer / pallet. A pallet (240 units) is ordered whenever the unrounded quantity falls within ±10% of 240 (216–264 units).

Price Determination

  • Uses SAP's standard condition technique to price a purchase order
Schema Determination → Condition Types (price, discounts/surcharges, freight, tax) → Condition Tables (key field combos) → Access Sequences (search order) → Condition Records (actual values + validity period, e.g. scaled vendor discounts)

Contracts & Release Orders

  • Contract: a long-term supplier agreement on articles and conditions, without fixed delivery dates or quantities
  • Quantity contract: targets a total order quantity over the validity period
  • Value contract: targets a total order value over the validity period
  • Site-specific contracts apply to one location; centrally agreed contracts apply across all sites in a purchasing org, with room for site-specific conditions
Contract → individual Contract Release Orders (POs referencing the contract) supply the actual delivery dates and quantities per shipment

Maintaining Order Confirmation and Goods Receipt Process in a Distribution Center

Order Confirmation

Confirmation Control

  • Defines the sequence in which expected confirmations (confirmation categories) are due to arrive
  • Specifies deadline, relevance to requirements planning/goods receipt, confirmation type (order confirmation, ASN, delivery note), and sequence when several are expected
  • Confirmation control key comes from vendor or article master data, or can be entered manually on an order item

Advanced Shipping Notifications (ASN)

  • Saving a PO triggers output messages (per customizing message types) covering content, transmission media (print/EDI/fax), and timing (immediate/periodic/application-driven)
  • Vendors use shipping notifications to announce delivery dates, times, quantities, and packing info
    Shipping notification → inbound delivery created in SAP Retail → serves as reference document for goods receipt

Goods Receipt Process

Process Structure

  • One-step: direct, complete goods receipt posting
  • Two-step: rough goods receipt (delivery note), then the actual goods receipt
  • Reference documents are typically the vendor PO or the shipping notification

Posting & Stock Types

  • Posting assigns merchandise to a storage location, a stock type, and (if WM is active) a specific storage bin
  • Unrestricted-use: no restrictions; quality inspection and blocked stock must move to unrestricted-use before withdrawal
  • Special stock indicators: vendor consignment, returnable transport packaging (RTP)
  • Referenced GR benefits: verifies delivery vs. order, suggests data to speed entry, auto-updates PO history, can auto-notify the purchaser

Inventory Management & Valuation

IM Types

  • Quantity and value basis: both stock quantity and value update, with G/L impact
  • Quantity basis only: only stock quantities update, no accounting data
  • Value-based IM: valuation at retail (sales) price for article groups; system derives the purchase value internally

Stock Value & Price Control

  • Stock shown at client, site, storage location, batch/valuation type, and special stock levels; advanced overview adds commitments (planned receipts/issues, open orders)
  • Standard price (S): fixed cost price — differences post to a price difference account
  • Moving average price (V): adjusts with receipt values and price changes
  • Price control is set on the article master's Accounting view, based on purchase or retail price
    Stock value = stock quantity × article price
  • Valuation at retail is optional for quantity-based IM but mandatory for value-based IM, and needs a revaluation profile when active
Order Confirmation & Goods Receipt SAP S/4HANA Retail — Distribution Center

Warehouse Management

Structure Components

  • Warehouse number: represents the whole warehouse structure
  • Storage types: areas with different handling requirements
  • Doors/staging areas: support goods receipt and issue
  • Storage sections: group bins with similar attributes
  • Storage bins: individual locations, 18-digit alphanumeric IDs
  • Quants: stored articles with attributes (receipt date, best-before date, batch, weight)
  • Activity areas/workstations: support capacity and labor calculations

WM Options in S/4HANA

  • MM-Inventory Management with no warehouse management
  • Lean WM with fixed bin storage
  • SAP Warehouse Management (WM) — supported until end of 2027
  • Extended Warehouse Management (EWM) — decentralized options
  • Embedded EWM (Basic or Advanced)

Goods Movements & Transaction Types

  • Goods receipt (GR): external receipt, increases warehouse stock
  • Goods issue (GI): shipment/withdrawal, decreases stock
  • Stock transfer: movement between storage locations or sites
  • Transfer posting: umbrella term for stock transfers and stock-identification changes
  • Movements are controlled by 3-digit movement types, which determine which reference documents (e.g. a PO for a goods receipt) apply

Value-Based Inventory Management

  • Goods movements are not posted per article — only on a retail-value basis, posted to a value-only article
  • Store master settings and merchandise categories determine which value-only article gets the posting
  • Fine-tuning is possible via group value-only articles within merchandise categories
  • Typically used in specific store scenarios, not in distribution centers

Executing Logistics Invoice Verification

Overview

  • The system builds an expected invoice from goods receipt quantities, articles, and PO conditions
  • The actual supplier invoice is compared against that target amount
  • If amounts match, or fall within tolerance, the invoice posts for payment — this is goods-receipt based invoice verification
  • Runs inside the Materials Management (MM) module, enabling decentralized verification rather than routing through Financial Accounting (FI)

Core Process Flow

Enter incoming invoice & assign PO/goods receipt (online or via EDI/IDoc) → Compare actual amount to target/expected amount → Post invoice once balance is zero or within tolerance → Transfer verified data to FI

Correction Handling

  • Discrepancies can be resolved by adjusting quantities, prices, or tax indicators, or by excluding a purchase order
  • Condition corrections trigger an automatic supplier notification via message type REKL
Logistics Invoice Verification SAP S/4HANA Retail — MM Module

Alternative Verification Methods

  • Assignment Test: preliminary check confirming an open goods receipt exists before full verification
  • Evaluated Receipt Settlement (ERS): target invoice built from GR quantities and PO conditions; amount is paid with no supplier invoice comparison
  • Prepayment Function: allows payment before verification, useful when cash discounts apply

Three Verification Types

  • Immediate online: requires item-level review as it happens
  • Immediate background: runs immediately, results reviewed afterward in a list
  • Scheduled background: runs on a schedule, allowing invoices to keep being created concurrently

Performing Condition Contract Settlement

Condition Contract Overview

  • A condition contract manages rebates for hitting quantity or value targets over a defined period
  • Holds the condition granter, special conditions (e.g. rebates), and can apply to one or more suppliers, customers, business partners, or materials
  • Maintained via transaction WCOCO, or the Fiori app "Maintain Contract – Condition Contracts" — the single entry point for contract data and its conditions

Header Section

  • Contract owner and validity period
  • Document status — determines what can still be modified
  • Currency and exchange rate information
  • Terms of payment and purchasing organization / sales area
  • Settlement type and settlement calendar
  • Business volume base — defined at Vendor, Vendor/Article, or Vendor/Site level
Condition Contract Settlement SAP S/4HANA Retail

Conditions Section

  • Stored in Customizing as condition tables
  • Made up of condition records with a type, access fields, and a validity period
  • Values are expressed as currency amounts or percentages
  • After entry, only the validity period and values can still be changed
  • Accruals are tracked as separate condition records

Settlement Process

  • WB2R_BUSVOL (Display Business Volume): view contract volumes at various levels of aggregation
  • WB2R_SV (Settle Condition Contracts): runs settlement for supplier contracts
  • The system calculates bonuses off scaled thresholds and adjusts accrual amounts as the settlement period progresses

Explaining Store Connection with Point-of-Sale Sales

POS Sales Capture

  • POS system: records retail sales at stores — scans article GTINs, determines pricing, captures quantities, processes payments, manages returns
  • Transaction Log (TLog): database of POS register transactions
  • Enables balance inquiries, employee monitoring, order management, customer service/dispute resolution
  • Accessible at both store and corporate level; supports compliance monitoring

POS Simulation

  • Manual entry of inbound POS transactions in SAP Retail — used to test configuration without connected POS hardware

SAP Retail POS Interface

  • Bidirectional master & transactional data exchange between central SAP Retail and store POS systems

Outbound (HQ → Store)

  • Article data, pricing, merchandise hierarchies, promotions, shipping notifications

Inbound (Store → HQ)

  • Sales & returns, financial transactions, goods movements, store receipts
    • POS capture → POS Inbound interface → updates store stock accounts → billing documents for Financial Accounting → payment data (card/customer card/cash) to Accounting

Store Connection Options

  • Option A — Direct SAP Retail–POS: HQ connects directly to store POS servers; sales data routes through SAP Customer Activity Repository and SAP POS DTA for validation before reaching SAP Retail
  • Option B — External Merchandise Management System: non-SAP store system communicates with central systems and updates local POS servers independently
  • Option C — SAP In-Store Merchandising: store operations integrate with central SAP Retail via SAP Fiori Store Operations apps; POS servers only record transactions
Store Connection & POS Sales SAP S/4HANA Retail — feeding Replenishment Planning

Data Transfer Methods

  • Traditional (on-premise): middleware — external converters or SAP PI/PO — using IDocs or RFC to convert between SAP IDoc format and POS formats
  • Service-based: used for specific transfers, e.g. bonus buy info via transaction code WESOUT
  • Cloud (SAP S/4HANA): Outbound via Data Replication Framework (DRFOUT, enterprise services); Inbound via SOAP-based services, monitored through SAP Application Interface Framework (AIF)

POS Outbound Processing

  • Initialization messages load data first time; later transfers send delta (changes only) to reduce bandwidth
  • Transfers articles/GTINs, price changes, promotions, merchandise hierarchies, bonus buys, POs/inventory docs (non-SAP systems)
  • Frequency & retention configurable in SAP Retail customizing

SAP POS DTA & SAP CAR

  • SAP POS Data Transfer and Audit (DTA): component of SAP CAR — loads granular receipt-level sales data, runs centralized sales audit (checks & corrections), distributes data at varying granularity to target systems
  • SAP Customer Activity Repository (CAR): collects granular multi-channel sales data enriched with master/transactional data; enables real-time stock visibility (unrestricted inventory + unprocessed POS sales)

Consuming Applications

  • SAP Promotion Management, SAP Replenishment Planning
  • SAP Merchandise Planning, SAP Assortment Planning, SAP Allocation Management

Supporting Platforms

  • Demand Data Foundation (DDF) — HANA-based data model; Unified Demand Forecasting (UDF) — combines Promotion Mgmt & Forecasting/Replenishment forecasts
  • Omnichannel: Promotion Pricing, Article Availability (Advanced ATP), On Shelf Availability

Generating Replenishment Planning for Stores

  • Replenishment: demand-oriented merchandise supply to recipients (stores/customers); auto-generates follow-on documents from stock situations
  • Vendor Managed Inventory (VMI): requirements planning run as a service — retailer needs access to customer's stock & sales data

Planning Types

  • RP: static (manual) target stock — standard/simplified
  • RF: dynamic target stock, needs forecast run (standard only)
  • RS: time-phased, static target stock + planning/delivery cycles
  • RR: time-phased, dynamic target stock

Target Stock & Procedures

  • Static: manual values in article master; for next/same-day delivery items
  • Dynamic: MRP forecast + target range of coverage, safety stock, min/max — not stored in master data
  • Standard procedure: full MM inventory management, per-article goods movements
  • Simplified procedure: value-only tracking based solely on POS sales quantities; static target stock only
    • Store POS sales → SAP CAR / POS DTA sales audit → SAP Replenishment Planning consumes sales & stock data → target stock & forecast calculation → follow-on replenishment documents to stores

Running Replenishment Planning

Replenishment Fundamentals

  • Replenishment: demand-oriented merchandise supply to recipients; requirements derived from stock situations, follow-on documents generated automatically
  • Store Replenishment (Rapid Replenishment): supplies retail locations
  • Customer Replenishment (VMI): supplies external customers; retailer accesses customer's stock & sales data

Learning Objectives

  • Explain foundational principles of Replenishment & Store Order functions
  • Execute Replenishment Planning processes
  • Differentiate the two inventory management types

Inventory Management Procedures

Standard Procedure

  • Detailed Materials Management (MM) tracking; each goods movement recorded per article document
  • Stocks managed on article basis
  • Supports static & dynamic target stock; full replenishment functionality with forecast

Simplified Procedure

  • Replenishment-Based Inventory Management — merchandise-category, value-only tracking
  • Based solely on POS sales quantities; no ATP checks, no forecast support
  • Static target stock determination only; for systems without full MM integration

Planning Types & Target Stock

  • RP: static target stock — standard/simplified, manual maintenance
  • RF: dynamic, forecast-driven — standard only, needs prior MRP forecast
  • RS: time-phased static — standard/simplified, planning/delivery cycles
  • RR: time-phased dynamic — standard only, forecast-driven targets

Target Stock Determination

  • Static: empirical value in article master (Logistics Store view); for same/next-day delivery articles; used with RP/RS
  • Dynamic: MRP forecast + target range of coverage (days) + safety stock, optional min/max; used with RF (or RR); result not saved, calculated per run

Article Fundamentals

  • Article (= Material/Product): smallest independently-orderable unit, touching the whole supply chain
  • Master record data: descriptive (text, dimensions, weight), control (article type, tax category), auto-updated (stock/valuation per site)
  • Article number: assigned internally (system range) or externally (manual range)
  • Article type controls: number ranges, value/quantity-based stock management, which data areas apply, external procurement vs. internal production — examples: HAWA (Trading Goods), VERP (Packaging), VOLL (Full Products), EMPT (Empties)
  • Merchandise category: mandatory grouping used in documents, search, and default-data referencing

Article Categories

  • Single article (00): traded individually, may exist in multiple units of measure
  • Generic article (01): abstract "style" header; its Variants differ only in characteristics (color/size) but share most other data

Structured Articles (Bill of Material)

  • Displays (12): bought together, sold individually, own presentation packaging
  • Sales sets (10): bought individually, sold together at a set price
  • Prepacks (11): variants of a generic article bought together, sold individually
    Empties (article type EMPT) link to Full Products (article type FULL) via an empties BOM per unit of measure

Units of Measure & GTIN

  • Base UoM: smallest sellable unit; used for inventory management
  • Order Unit: default for purchasing info records (overridable per record)
  • Sales Unit: default for store distribution chains
  • Delivery/Issue Unit: default for DC/wholesale chains, maintained per site
    Example: Piece (base) → Box = 4 pc (sales) → Layer = 16 pc (delivery) → Pallet = 64 pc (order)
  • GTIN (successor to EAN/UPC since 2009; "EAN" still common in practice): defined per unit of measure, not per article; GTIN categories control number ranges, check digits, prefixes

Managing Assortments (3I5) · 12 min

Assortment Management Basics

  • Central question: which articles, in which stores, during which period?
  • Assortment Modules connect assigned articles with assigned stores
  • A local (site-specific) assortment is auto-created once a site's Business Partner (customer data) is fully maintained
  • Example: a DIY chain and a supermarket chain need different articles routed to their respective stores

Creating Modules & Versions

  • Manage Assortment Modules app (F4892): central app for modules, versions, article assignments, and store assignments
  • Modules can be organized by shelf/area, merchandise category, sales-floor presentation, or custom business rules
  • Each module has ≥1 version, each with its own validity period — gaps allowed, no overlaps, minimum 1 day
    Example: an "outdoor toys" module versions to snow gear in winter, beach gear in summer
  • Articles are assigned to specific module versions, each with its own assignment period

Listing Conditions

  • Listing = defining the time-dependent article–site relationship
  • A valid listing condition is required before a site can order or post goods receipt for an article
  • Sales period lives in the article master; procurement period comes from assortment assignment periods
  • If an unlisted article is received, the system checks whether "subsequent listing" is allowed for that site
  • Listing also triggers site-specific article data in the background (valuation, replenishment settings)

Performing Sales Pricing for Retail (3I4) · 12 min

Pricing Fundamentals

  • Extends assortment's question with: at which price?
  • Each article typically needs several prices: retail, wholesale, DC transfer, possibly e-commerce — at distribution-chain, price-list, or site/customer level
  • The system calculates sales prices from settings rather than requiring fully manual entry
  • Other conditions (discounts, freight, tax) exist too, but this lesson focuses on sales price calculation

Pricing Levels & Promotions

  • 3 pricing levels: Distribution Chain, Distribution Chain + Price List, Site-Specific
    Determination always picks the most detailed price available, falling back level by level
  • Promotion prices override regular prices for their validity period, then regular pricing resumes
  • All prices/conditions are time-dependent, so even the "regular" price can change between periods

Basic Price Calculation

  • Step 1: determine a cost price (from purchase conditions or current valuation)
  • Step 2: add the planned markup % (condition type EDS1), maintained per merchandise category for a chain/price list
    Cost price → + planned markup (EDS1) → final price → optional price-point rounding
  • Price Point Groups control rounding (e.g. end in "9"); configurable by chain/price list, optionally by category, and can differ for regular vs. promotion prices
  • Create Sales Price Calculation app (VKP5): list groups A/B (header+item) or C/D (ALV table view)
Sales Pricing 3I4 · Basic Concepts

One-Step vs. Two-Step Calculation

  • One-step: store price calculated directly from the external vendor's purchase price (no DC involved)
  • Two-step: store receives via a DC — system first calculates the DC transfer price, then uses that as the store's cost basis
  • Multi-step is also possible (e.g. regional DC → local DC)
  • Controlled by the supply source key in the article's logistics data: external → one-step, internal → two-step

PPDS & SPDS

Purchase Price Determination

  • 01 basic/regular vendor · 02 sales-set virtual price · 03 perishables average · 04 manual entry · 07 combines 01–03

Sales Price Determination

  • 01 markup calc · 02 fixed/existing price (backwards-calculates markup) · 03 suggested price · 04/05 mass % or absolute change · 06 combines 02+01

Pricing Control & Generic Article Pricing

  • Pricing Type (config): controls pricing procedure, currency, conversion rate, and pricing reference site
  • Pricing Documents: statuses A/B (calculated, unreleased) → C (released/active) → D (flagged for deletion) → E (cancelled, reverts to prior price)
  • Pricing Worklist: auto-monitors purchase-price and supply-source changes, flagging articles for recalculation

Generic Article Pricing Profiles

  • Blank: priced individually · 1: generic price suggested, editable per variant · 2: one price, inherited, non-editable
    Var.Pr.All indicator allows individual promotion prices per variant even under profile 2

Executing Replenishment Planning for Stores (3I7)

Replenishment Overview

  • Demand-oriented merchandise supply for stores, usually run as a scheduled background job
  • Calculates demand at article / site level
  • Uses an ATP check for the stock situation plus factors like planned delivery time
  • Generates purchase requisitions as follow-on documents
  • Executed with the Rapid Replenishment function
Flow: maintain parameters (RP or RF) → forecast run if RF → replenishment run → purchase requisitions → purchase order (external) or stock transfer order (internal)

RP vs RF Planning Types

RP – Static Target Stock

  • Fills up to a static target stock when expected stock falls below the reorder point
  • Reorder point is optional but helps avoid piece-by-piece replenishment; safety stock is optional too
  • Good fit for articles with same-day/next-day delivery

RF – Dynamic Target Stock

  • Requires a forecast run before replenishment
  • Target range of coverage (in workdays, per the store's factory calendar) sets the dynamic target stock
  • Optional min/max target stock bounds; the dynamic value itself isn't stored in the article master

Forecasting

  • Forecast parameters: period indicator, number of historical values, number of forecast periods
  • 4 models: Constant, Trend, Seasonal, Seasonal Trend
  • Each model needs a minimum number of consumption values; extra values trigger an ex-post accuracy check
  • New articles/sites can reference another article's or site's history
Apps: Schedule Material Demand Forecast Runs (F2464) for immediate/recurring runs; manual forecast in Maintain Article (MM42) for testing
Replenishment Planning for Stores (3I7) SAP S/4HANA Cloud Retail · Store Replenishment Unit

Purchasing Model & Article Setup

  • Supports centralized purchasing (head office only) and local purchasing autonomy
  • Store staff can review/adjust proposed quantities in the Order Products app before a daily cutoff (e.g. 10 am)
  • Supply source indicator decides the follow-on document: external vendor PO, internal stock transfer order (DC), or a priority between the two
  • RP/RF, ATP check rule, planned delivery time, and GR processing time are set in the Logistics Store view of Maintain Article (MM42)

Requirement Groups

  • Enable mass maintenance of target/min/max stock, reorder point, and safety stock across many stores
  • Groups (e.g. small / medium / large demand) are assigned per merchandise category to a site
  • Site-level values can still be set as protected exceptions that won't be overwritten
Example: stores with "small demand" for dairy might target 40 EA for milk but 20 EA for yoghurt — same group, different articles

Execution & Monitoring Apps

  • Execute Rapid Replenishment (WRP1R): run replenishment directly
  • Schedule Rapid Replenishment Run (F5478): regular background job, e.g. overnight
  • Monitor Replenishment (WRMO): review successful items and errors; jump to follow-on purchase requisitions
  • Display Replenishment Parameter (WR60): overview of RP/RF settings, stock, and run status
Example: forecast 100 PC/week (20 PC/day), ATP 0, 1-day lead time, 3-day target range of coverage → a Monday run delivers Tuesday and orders 60 PC (covering Tue–Thu)
Public holidays: weekly demand is redistributed across the remaining workdays instead of being lost (e.g. 25 PC/day instead of 20 across 4 remaining days)

Performing In-Store Inventory Management (3HR)

Physical Inventory Fundamentals

  • Physical Inventory (PI): legally required; balances physical stock against book figures at least once a year
  • Periodic PI: a full count at or near fiscal year-end (sometimes half-yearly/quarterly)
  • Continuous PI: parts of the stock counted across the year — every article counted at least once
  • Cycle counting: site-level indicators set the count frequency per article, tied to ABC analysis — fast movers counted more often
  • Most retailers combine periodic and continuous counting

The 4-Phase Process

  • Prepare → Count & enter → Analyze results → Post differences
    Prepare Physical Inventory → Count and Enter Stock → Accept or Recount → Post Inventory Differences
  • Phase 1: documents are printed, loaded to a mobile/handheld device, or processed via a Fiori app
  • Phase 2: count data is entered or loaded; the system calculates the difference vs. book balance
  • Phase 3: the manager reviews results and may trigger a recount; accepted documents move to "counted" status
  • Phase 4: differences post to stock quantity/value and to the FI stock accounts / material ledger

Creating & Managing PI Documents

  • Create Documents – Physical Inventory (WV31): mass creation by site; can exclude zero-stock or already-counted articles; can limit to storage locations or merchandise categories
  • Create Physical Inventory Documents (F3197): targeted creation by article, storage location, and stock type
  • Manage Physical Inventory Documents (F0379A): view documents and post differences
  • A PI document holds: site/storage location, planned count date, articles & stock types to count, and item/document count status

Processing Merchandise Distribution & Collective PO (71O)

Merchandise Distribution Basics

  • Happens at the distribution center using a Pull planning process
  • Store (DC recipient) requirements are bundled into Collective Purchase Orders
  • Procurement documents and issue documents are linked and stored as distribution data
  • Parties involved: the supplier, the distribution center, and the stores

Push vs. Pull Planning

  • Push: create an allocation table referencing an existing procurement document, then generate the issue documents (the 5FL approach)
  • Pull: combine existing stock transfer order and/or sales order quantities into one collective PO to the supplier, at article/DC level
Push = plan first, order later · Pull = requirements already exist, bundle them into one order

Processing Methods

  • Processing starts with goods receipt in the DC and ends with goods issue
  • Three basic methods: cross-docking, flow-through, putaway
  • Two optimizing procedures: cross-docking/flow-through, cross-docking/putaway
  • Cross-docking is leanest: only goods receipt and issue are required, other steps can be automated
Benefits: lower handling costs, reduced stock and capital lockup, faster flow of goods

02 · Management Accounting

Where costs and results are measured

03 · Purchasing Organization

The commercial buying unit

  • Responsibility: Procures articles, negotiates supplier terms, and owns the purchasing transaction. One purchasing transaction is processed within one purchasing organization.

04 · Buying Teams & Shared Terms

People and reporting

  • Purchasing group: One buyer or a team responsible for day-to-day purchasing. It is not a separate level for retaining purchasing master data.

05 · Sales Units & Sales Area

Build the sales combination

  • Sales organization: The selling unit responsible for sales conditions and product liability. Each belongs to exactly one company code; a company code may have several.

07 · Sites & Inventory Valuation

The operational location

  • Site: A store, distribution center, or production location. This is where replenishment is planned and merchandise stocks are managed.
  • Company-code assignment: Every site belongs to exactly one company code. A company code can contain many sites.

08 · Inventory & Warehouse Detail

Layers of physical detail

  • Inventory Management (IM): Tracks stock at site/storage-location level. A site may have several storage locations.
  • Warehouse number: Connects a site/storage-location combination to warehouse management. One warehouse can cover multiple such combinations.
  • Inside the warehouse: Storage types organize areas; storage bins identify precise positions. This adds physical detail beneath the inventory-management view.

Understand the lesson’s warehouse terms

  • IM only: No warehouse number is needed when stock is managed only at storage-location level.
  • Lean WM: Supports picking with transfer orders but does not hold inventory quantities by storage bin.

01Does a new sales channel automatically need a new company code?

No. The channel describes the selling route; the company code defines the accounting boundary. Review both requirements separately.

02What is missing from a distribution chain to make a sales area?

The division. Chain = sales organization + channel; sales area adds division.

03Can a purchasing group replace a purchasing organization?

No. The group identifies the buyer/team; the organization carries purchasing responsibility and organizational data.

04Does a second storage location create a second valuation area?

No. Both remain within the same site; SAP Retail valuation is at site level.

05Does Lean WM track the stock quantity in every bin?

No. Inventory remains at storage-location level even though picking can use transfer orders.

Compare the required distribution-chain view, permitted assignments, and any reference settings. Then investigate the specific process error.
